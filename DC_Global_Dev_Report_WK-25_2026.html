Week 25 is anchored by Seattle's June 9 unanimous passage of Council Bill 121214 -- a 1-year DC moratorium (extendable +6 months) covering data centers exceeding 20 megavolt-amperes. The Seattle Council Blog reported the vote; GeekWire confirmed Councilmembers received more than 98,000 emails on the issue; >50 residents testified at the meeting and not one spoke in favor; Gizmodo called the action "the most symbolically potent data center moratorium yet"; KOMO, KING5, MyNorthwest, Common Dreams, TechRadar, Tom's Hardware and Techstory all carried the story. The week's second pillar landed June 11: the Stop Data Centers Coalition launched with more than 500 advocacy groups demanding Congress pass a federal moratorium (Common Dreams). Lexington-Fayette Urban County (KY) unanimously passed its own moratorium June 9 pausing permits, development plans and zone changes through Oct 31; Davidson County / Nashville Metro Council advanced a temporary moratorium June 10 on the first of three required readings; West Haven CT approved a 1-year ban; Spokane WA introduced a 1-year emergency moratorium with the vote scheduled today June 15; Lincoln Township MI passed a 10-month pause; South Strabane Township PA adopted DC + noise ordinances with a 1,500-ft residential setback; Pasco County FL Planning Commission recommended a 1-year pause after a standing-room-only Dade City hearing; Allegany County NY voted to preserve land for housing over data center use (Supervisor McPherson citing "few permanent jobs while consuming large amounts of electricity"); Murray KY sent draft DC regulations back for revisions; and Arkansas's Pulaski County Quorum Court pushed its 12-month moratorium decision to July after a tense meeting that drew nearly 100 attendees. Project pipeline still adds: Goochland County VA received an application June 11 for "Tuckahoe Tech Park" -- a 900-acre, 12-building DC campus; Hyperscale Data Inc bought 48.5 acres in southwest Michigan to expand its DC campus; Kasi Cloud Datacenters cut the ribbon on its Lekki Lagos campus, what it calls West Africa's first hyperscale-ready, AI-capable, carrier-neutral DC platform; NextDC topped out KL1, its first international Tier IV facility (65 MW) in Kuala Lumpur, which has already achieved Uptime Institute Tier IV design certification and a Green Building Index Platinum rating; and Equinix committed $190M to KL2 in Cyberjaya, powered by renewable energy from day one. Brockovich's map was refreshed June 9 with new community-reported pins continuing to compound (I re-fetched brockovichdatacenter.com -- the "Last updated: June 9, 2026" stamp is live and the existing six concern categories -- energy, water, e-waste, location risk, scalability, noise -- remain the anchor of the page).
