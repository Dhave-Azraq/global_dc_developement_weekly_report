Week 38 is defined by politics catching up with power bills in the US, and by Africa and APAC posting their strongest single week of the year. In the United States the backlash became legislative: the House is set to vote on a bipartisan bill to stop data centres driving up household electricity costs, with supporters arguing that households should not be forced to subsidise the infrastructure required by large commercial data centres. Utilities requested $18.6 billion in rate increases in the first half of 2026 alone, much of that spending on power plants and grid upgrades built to serve data-centre electricity demand. North Carolina, Ohio, Illinois, New York and Virginia are seeing the most direct impact, from Duke Energy's 9.5% two-year residential increase in NC to a projected $70 per month increase for Ohio families by 2028. At least 18 states have introduced bills creating special rate classes for large energy users, and new state-level proposals aim to ensure facilities pay the full cost of grid connections and participate in demand-response programmes, preventing cost shifts to residential customers. Governors travelled to the White House to unveil a deal to curb power prices. The Congressional Research Service published "Data Centers and the Electricity Grid" on September 1. Energy Connects reports that opposition has escalated from a niche local issue to a defining topic ahead of the November midterm elections, with as many as half of the proposed data centres in the US at risk of delay or cancellation. On the finance side, the largest private investment in digital infrastructure to date closed: a consortium of the AI Infrastructure Partnership (AIP), Abu Dhabi's MGX and BlackRock's Global Infrastructure Partners acquired Aligned Data Centers for approximately $40 billion, completing the transaction on July 21, 2026. The deal expands Aligned's 50-campus, 5 GW platform across the Americas, targeting key land, power and equipment bottlenecks in AI buildouts. The consortium plans to invest $30 billion in equity initially with potential to scale to $100 billion including debt, plus $5 billion in additional capital for expansion. AIP was founded by BlackRock, GIP, MGX, Microsoft and NVIDIA; Aligned remains headquartered in Dallas under CEO Andrew Schaap. Africa recorded its strongest week of 2026. Vodafone Business, Cassava Technologies and Elsewedy Electric signed an agreement to establish Africa Data Centers Egypt, a project that will initially provide 20 megawatts of capacity over three years with plans to expand to 200 MW. The ministry expects the venture to attract US$200 million in foreign direct investment initially, growing to US$1 billion upon completion, and to create up to 200 direct and 2,000 indirect jobs. The project includes Egypt's first sovereign AI data centre powered by NVIDIA GPUs, delivered through Cassava as NVIDIA's official cloud partner in Africa, and will offer GPU-as-a-Service to support large-scale AI applications in Egypt, keeping sensitive data and AI workloads onshore rather than sending them to servers outside the country. Separately, on September 14, Digital Parks Africa expanded its footprint into West Africa with the development of Nigeria Data Centre 1 (NDC1), a new carrier-neutral facility in Lagos. Datacloud Africa 2026 ran in Nairobi co-located with ITW Africa, bringing together operators, hyperscalers, energy providers, investors and technology partners around AI expansion, power strategy, data-centre finance and hyperscale growth. Across the GCC, Humain — the Public Investment Fund-owned AI company — partnered with DataVolt to develop the first 100 MW phase of a 360 MW data centre in NEOM's Oxagon industrial region, slated to be ready in 2028. Khazna announced plans to expand operational capacity by more than 1 GW over the next five years across the Middle East and Europe, with more than 400 MW of new capacity delivered in key international markets including Saudi Arabia and Italy, alongside a 100 MW AI-optimised data centre in Ajman with its first phase expected online by December 2026. A Saudi company launched a dedicated fund to build two data centres. In Qatar, Ooredoo has started work on a new data centre at Mesaimeer; the facility uses energy-efficient cold-aisle containment technology that helps drive up to four times greater heat and power densities than standard data centres, and Ooredoo's data centre holds ISO 22301 Business Continuity Management Systems accreditation. Ooredoo's five facilities now total roughly 55,000 sq ft, funded by the QAR 2 billion landmark financing deal with QNB, Doha Bank and Masraf Al Rayan, while Ooredoo's Syntys acquired Q Data to expand its Qatar portfolio. MEEZA's QAR 1 billion-plus (~$270 million) 8 MW hyperscaler anchor lease from WK-37 remains the market's largest single contract of its kind, with M-VAULT 4 at Qatar Science & Technology Park and the second Dukhan Bank QAR 1.6 billion ($439 million) facility for M-Vault 6, 7 and 8 funding the build. In the UK it was a policy week with a hard deadline: the grid-regulations consultation closes September 16. The revised National Planning Policy Framework published on August 17 strengthens planning support for AI Growth Zones, data centres and the infrastructure needed to support growth sectors, and requires planning strategy to recognise data-driven industries and associated infrastructure more explicitly. Government policy papers describe timely electricity connections as the single biggest blocker to establishing AI Growth Zones, proposing faster connections for strategic schemes, removal of speculative demand, and mechanisms that could reserve or reallocate grid capacity. The UK will explore nuclear power for its new AI data centre plan, and government has rejected calls for a data-centre construction freeze. A UK Data Center Portfolio outlook published September 14 flags expansion driven by cloud, AI and hyperscale demand. Microsoft's capital expenditure commitments include construction of the UK's largest supercomputer building in partnership with Nscale at its AI Campus in Loughton — though the 90 MW grid connection is still not ready for the planned 2027 opening, keeping Bloom Energy solid-oxide fuel-cell talks alive. In Asia, Japan overtook India for second place in APAC, adding 293 MW in H1 2026 to reach roughly 1.8 GW and moving ahead of India as the region's second-largest operational market; Soya Green Data Center 1 is Japan's first data centre directly connected to wind power. Johor, Malaysia holds the top spot in Cushman & Wakefield's APAC data-centre maturity index as of H1 2026, with combined operational and future capacity exceeding 4 GW, and AirTrunk's JHB3 and JHB4 form an RM12 billion two-campus investment purpose-built to support high-density cloud and AI workloads with recycled-water cooling systems. In India, Yotta Data Services is deploying 20,736 liquid-cooled NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra GPUs at its 60 MW D2 hyperscale campus, described as one of Asia's largest AI superclusters. Singapore's two capacity tranches totalling 1.2 GW keep the city-state premium for specialised AI workloads, while mainland China remains a fairly closed market dominated by domestic workloads. On hardware, NVIDIA Rubin is in full production with partner availability in H2 2026 across AWS, Google Cloud, Azure and OCI plus CoreWeave, Lambda, Nebius and Nscale. Qualcomm's AI200 ships in 2026 with 768 GB of LPDDR memory onboard, direct liquid cooling and a 160 kW per-rack envelope, with AI250 following in 2027 using near-memory computing for more than 10x effective memory bandwidth. AMD Helios (72 MI455X + 18 EPYC Venice + Pensando + ROCm, around $5.25 million per rack) ships through Q3 and H2 2026. Bosch Rexroth's Sytronix SvP/SvE variable-speed pump drives keep their Power & UPS slot, and with 18 states drafting special large-load rate classes and local 50 dBA noise caps spreading, the up to 20 dB(A) reduction now works as a dual regulatory lever — a tariff argument and a permitting argument at once.
