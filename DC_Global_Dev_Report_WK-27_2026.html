Week 27 is the systemic-risk inflection of 2026. Tom's Hardware confirms more than 75 US data-center build-outs worth ~$130B were blocked in the first three months of 2026 alone -- a bipartisan opposition wave organized around soaring power + water costs. Data Center Watch's tracker now puts $64B of DC projects blocked or delayed amid local opposition; TechRadar (citing the same dataset) writes that nearly half of US DCs planned to come online in 2026 will be delayed or canceled, against a baseline of approximately 12 GW. Of that 12 GW only ~4 GW is under active construction; the remaining two-thirds is pre-construction and exposed to further cancellation, repricing, or indefinite postponement. SemiAnalysis and Tech-Insider both flag a new supply-chain chokepoint: busways -- the heavy-current bus duct distributing power from switchgear to rack rows -- have entered the long-lead category for the first time, joining transformers, switchgear, and battery-backup systems. OpenAI's $500B Stargate Project has reportedly stalled in Texas, falling behind its original timeline. Sightline Climate is now directly tracking 9 outright DC cancellations in its 2026 dataset. The political layer is now visible: Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez introduced the federal Artificial Intelligence Data Center Moratorium Act on June 25 (DCD), defining AI DCs as facilities >20 MW with racks >20 kW, liquid-cooled, used for the development or operation of AI models at scale. NY Gov. Kathy Hochul has the Responsible Data Center Development Act (S10642/A11560) on her desk -- the bill passed the Senate 44-16 and the Assembly 102-39 in the final hours of the 2026 session, and she has a 10-day decision window; if signed, NY becomes the first state to enact a statewide moratorium and ~$10B of NY pipeline is at risk (Bisnow). The Hill (June 24) reported that Utah Senate President Stuart Adams lost his primary in a historic defeat tied explicitly to DC voter backlash, the most visible political consequence of the anti-DC wave to date. Virginia revised its DC permitting guidance, Oklahoma's "Data Center Consumer Ratepayer Protection Act" became law, and North Carolina advanced legislation preventing utilities from absorbing AI-infra cost risk. The hardware catalog continues to track Qualcomm AI200 (2026, 768 GB LPDDR onboard, 160 kW/rack DLC) and AI250 (2027, near-memory compute, >10x effective memory bandwidth) -- both anchoring the inference TCO challenge to NVIDIA/AMD with a 200 MW initial deployment target. Bosch Rexroth Sytronix variable-speed pump drives remain under Power & UPS for DC mechanical-services efficiency (cooling pumps, generator auxiliaries) and the up to 20 dB(A) noise-reduction angle that directly answers the Brockovich noise-concern category and recent setback ordinances. UK pipeline accelerates against the US risk wave: the UK Government approved a major Slough greenbelt DC on appeal (Berkshire) reinforcing "Europe's AI front-runner" status; Kao Data acquired the 4.7-acre Frogmore Industrial Estate (Park Royal, West London) for a new build; CBRE UK forecasts London new-supply 2025-26 at 373 MW -- more than 2x the prior 2-year 147 MW total. Microsoft NScale Loughton $15B, Google £5bn, Blackstone £10bn Blyth, and the North Wales AI Growth Zone all continue to advance. Capital deployment also extends: Avaio plans a $6B Little Rock Arkansas DC campus; TikTok's $29B Thailand investment plans were approved; Gorilla Korat 200 MW Thailand, CDC Australia 555 MW, and Kasi Cloud Lekki Lagos all proceed. Brockovich's map was refreshed June 24 -- the site now has a new "Communities & Legislation" tab and adds The Hill (June 24) Utah piece to its featured news.
